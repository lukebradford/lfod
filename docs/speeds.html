Diagonal

Straight ahead, a ship can keep up with light. Along a diagonal it can’t. Draw a line at forty-five degrees through the live cell farthest along the diagonal. A dead cell two steps beyond that line touches only one cell on it, and birth takes two, so the line can advance at most one step each generation. That is one diagonal step every two generations, and the shaded corner is beyond it. The ram ship, the smallest known diagonal ship, goes half as fast as that limit allows: one diagonal step every four generations.

The ram ship is its glossary pattern, simulated exactly on an unbounded plane with a fixed camera, one generation per step. It starts over after 48 diagonal steps. Click it to pause.
