Live Free or Die Universe

An exploration of the Moore neighborhood s0/b2 cellular automaton.

Live Free or Die, like Conway’s Game of Life, is a two-dimensional two-state Moore neighborhood cellular automaton. In other words, it is an infinite grid of cells which can either be live or dead. Given an initial configuration of cells, each cell applies simple rules based on its eight neighboring cells to determine whether its state will change. A live cell will die if it has any neighbors, and a dead cell will come to life if it has exactly two neighbors. Every cell in the grid changes at the same instant. As the pattern evolves through consecutive generations, it exhibits emergent and often beautiful behavior.

A short explanation

I found Live Free or Die by accident while messing around with simple variations of Seeds (/b2). It is so named because the only cells that survive between generations are free of neighbors. What first intrigued me about it were the extremely common free ship, the double and 2-2 dot puffers, and the key ship.

Live Free or Die is a small, fast, intuitive universe, in which most objects are more reminiscent of machinery than of the organic fluidity that is characteristic of Conway’s Life (s23/b3). It is full of readily visible patterns, and flotillas and other large projects are easy to assemble. The explosive nature of the rule makes it receptive to the naturalistic study of mobile objects. It has oscillators of each period and spaceships of each speed that is possible in the Seeds universe, along with objects that are impossible or undiscovered in Seeds: stable fuses, oscillators with stators, and oscillators and spaceships of indefinitely large period. In Live Free or Die, unlike Seeds, it seems that there is the potential to create anything that is possible in Life.
